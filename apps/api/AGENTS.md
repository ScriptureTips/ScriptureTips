# @scripturetips/api

The API: NestJS on Fastify, under `/api`. So far it serves translations and passages from the database (packages/db), accounts, compilations and publications.

- `GET /api/translations`: the translations that have text loaded.
- `GET /api/translations/{id}/passages?ref=Joel 2:28-32`: the verses of one or more references, read in the translation's numbering (@scripturetips/reference), each with its canonical passage, a display label, headings, markup and translator's notes (domain-model §2.6). At most `MAX_VERSES` (500) per request.
- `GET /api/translations/{id}/books`: the books with text, in registry order, each with its chapters (the reader arranges them by canon).
- **Accounts** (`src/auth/`, vision §11 "Sign-in"): Better Auth under `/api/auth/*`, mounted on Fastify by `AuthModule`. Sign-in is a one-time email code (6 digits, 10 minutes, 3 attempts, stored hashed) with **no passwords**; a new address becomes an account on its first sign-in. Asking for a code (`POST /api/auth/email-otp/send-verification-otp`) needs a solved ALTCHA bot check in the `x-bot-check` header, and is limited to 5 codes an hour per address and 60 requests an hour per IP. Then `POST /api/auth/sign-in/email-otp` sets the session cookie (30 days, renewed daily). Rate limits and spent bot-check solutions live in Valkey (`KeyStore`). Limits per IP read the client address from `X-Forwarded-For`, which the web app's proxy passes on: **in production the reverse proxy in front of the web app must overwrite that header with the client's address**, or clients could choose their own. Without it (as in development), every request shares one bucket; email goes over SMTP (`Mailer`, Mailpit in development).
- `GET /api/account/bot-check`: a bot-check puzzle. `GET /api/account`: the signed-in user (email, handle, display name, locale, canon profile, trust level). `PUT /api/account`: set the handle (once, for now; checked against domain-model §1.6 and a reserved list), display name (checked against the reserved names in any script) canon profile (`@scripturetips/canon`, no default, changeable) and country (a code from `@scripturetips/countries`, or `null`; anything else is `country-invalid`). `countryNotListed` (2 to 100 characters, instead of `country`) records a claim that their country isn't on the list, in `country_claim`, and clears the country; the account's own view returns it as `countryClaim`, no one else's does, and nothing shows it until it's reviewed (vision §11).
- **Compilations** (`src/compilations/`, domain-model §2.3), private to their owner: every route needs a session, and slugs are looked up among the signed-in user's own (someone else's is `unknown-compilation`, 404). `GET`/`POST /api/compilations` lists and creates (title, abbreviation, base translation); `GET`/`DELETE /api/compilations/{slug}`; `POST /api/compilations/{slug}/rules` adds a rule (`{scope: {section} | {reference}, source}`); `DELETE …/rules/{id}` resets it to inherited; `GET …/books` and `GET …/passages?ref=` read it like a translation. Each verse carries its `source` (and `edit`, `correction` or `adaptation`, where its wording was changed), and the result lists the `sources` to credit. `GET …/composition` counts every verse by source; the count is kept in the key store (Valkey) for a day under a hash of what it depends on (base, rules and single verses), so it's counted once per change.
  - **Single verses:** `GET …/verses?ref=` shows one verse as the compilation has it beside every translation's that may be taken; `POST …/verses` (`{reference, source, text?, kind?, changeNote?}`) takes it from `source`, or with `text` saves an edit of that source's wording; `DELETE …/verses/{id}` resets it to inherited. Every save and reset is kept in `compilation_verse_revision`.
  - **Edits** (`wording.ts`): the user edits plain text, and a word-level diff carries the change back into the source's markup (styles, idiom spans, note marks, breaks; a changed word loses its Strong's attributes). Within the license's `correctionLimit` (3 words or punctuation marks; spacing isn't counted) it's a correction unless the user calls it an adaptation; beyond it, an adaptation. Refused under `no_modification`. The edit keeps a snapshot of the source verse (headings, content, notes) with its hash and import, and shows the snapshot's headings and notes; the editor flags `sourceChanged` when the source verse no longer matches.
  - **Own renderings** (domain-model §2.3.1, `authorship.service.ts`, `journal.ts`, `similarity.ts`): `POST …/journals` (`{reference}`) starts a writing session (from the compilation's own wording, or empty) and notes the translations the editor shows as the study trail; `POST …/journals/{id}/events` adds bursts of writing (`{kind, position, removed, inserted, elapsed?}`), each checked against the text so far and hash-chained to the one before. A paste of at least three words (or a whole verse) from a translation the editor shows is `internal`; any other paste is `external`. `POST …/verses` with `{reference, journal, declaration?}` saves the session's text: an edit of a translation it pasted from (the closest), else `own` with the declaration (the `AUTHORSHIP_STATEMENT` version the user was shown), else `pasted` if it pasted from outside, else refused (`declaration-required`). Own and pasted verses record their similarity to every translation; `POST …/verses/{id}/declaration` declares a pasted one. `GET …/verses/{id}/evidence` downloads the evidence bundle with each journal checked (not yet signed or externally timestamped). Choosing a translation's wording again, or a reset, deletes the verse's evidence.
  - **Translator's notes** (domain-model §2.3, `notes.ts`): an edited verse shows its source snapshot's notes, and an own rendering none, as changed by `compilation_note` rows. `POST …/notes` (`{reference, change}`) makes one change: `{add: {text, after}}` adds an own note marked after the verse's word `after` (at most 20); `{position, text}` rewords a note (a source note becomes `modified`, or unchanged again when reworded as it was; the body keeps its styles and `fr` reference); `{position, after}` moves an own note; `{position, removed: true}` leaves a source note out (recorded) or deletes an own one; `{position, restore: true}` brings back the source's note. A note belongs to its verse: changing one on a verse taken unchanged first makes it an edited verse of its translation (a correction, words unchanged), if the license allows changes. Each change is a revision with the notes. A new edit of the same source keeps the changes to notes it still has; another source keeps only own notes; taking a translation's wording unchanged drops them all. The passage API marks changed notes with `origin` (`modified`, `own`); own notes are in the evidence export.
- **Publications** (`src/publications/`, domain-model §2.3): `POST /api/compilations/{slug}/publication` (`{visibility: unlisted | public, changeNote?}`) publishes the compilation as its next version: a full snapshot of every verse resolved as the reader has it (`CompilationsService.snapshot`, a whole Bible in a few seconds), stored a chapter per row (`publication_chapter`) with the rest in the version's `meta` (title, credits, sources and how many verses each gave, under which license). Each version has a random 10-character public ID, a number, and a content hash over its meta, every chapter's hash and the previous version's hash; the change summary counts the verses changed since the previous version. The first publish creates the publication under the compilation's slug, kept (with its slug) if the compilation is deleted (`slug-published`). Publishing follows domain-model §1.4: `GET …/publication/check` runs the automated license check (`checks.ts`, pure: problems, similarity warnings, the license composition, the attributions to show) and names the consent statement's version; `POST` needs that `consent` number (`consent-outdated`), records a `publish_request`, re-runs the check, and either returns it (`publish-returned` with the problems) or publishes, recorded as reviewed only by the license check. Each version's meta keeps its attributions, and `…/passages` returns them. The age declaration and content review come later. `GET …/publication` is the owner's view with every version. Reading needs no account: `GET /api/publications/{handle}/{slug}/books` and `…/passages?ref=`, each at the latest version of a public publication, or at `?version={id}` for any published version; an unlisted publication is read only at a pinned version (`open` is the one access check). A pinned version tells its reader the latest version's number when the publication is public. Both reads are cached by readers for 60 seconds and revalidated by an ETag (a bodiless 304 when unchanged); not yet immutable for a pinned version, since the response also names the latest version.
  - **Resolution** (`resolve.ts`): the verse list and numbering come from the base; for each verse the most specific rule wins (range, chapter, book, section), matched through the canonical scheme. A source verse is used only when it covers exactly the same canonical verses; otherwise the next layer supplies it, down to the base.
  - **Rules:** one reference, whole verses, within the base's text; no overlap with a rule of the same kind; no single verse (that's a single-verse choice). Licenses are checked as data (`@scripturetips/licenses`): `not_as_base` and `no_modification` so far, and an unknown license key allows nothing.
  - **Not yet:** books the base lacks can't take rules (that needs the scheme's own verse lists), and a compilation's language and direction are its base's.
- **PDF builds** (`src/pdf-builds/`, book-designer.md §8, §9), private to the compilation's owner: `POST /api/compilations/{slug}/pdf-builds` (`{scope?}`: books or chapters, whole chapters, e.g. "Ruth"; the whole compilation without it) describes the book (packages/book, from `CompilationsService.snapshot` limited to those books), stores its `book.json` in the file store (packages/storage) and queues it for the PDF worker (apps/pdf-worker) on BullMQ. Only the license problems that forbid any use or change stop a build (`build-not-allowed`), since a build is for the user's own use; editions will check what may be given away or sold. One build at a time per user (`build-in-progress`) and `PDF_DAILY_BUILDS` a day (`daily-build-limit`, default 10; vision §7.1). `GET …/pdf-builds` lists the latest 20, bringing queued and running ones up to date from the queue (a job the queue has lost is failed as `lost`). `GET …/pdf-builds/{id}/{file}` downloads `book.pdf`, `cover.pdf`, `book.json`, `book.typ` or `cover.typ` (a failed build keeps its `book.json`). Deleting a compilation deletes its builds' files first.
- **Roles** (`API_ROLE`, vision §7.2): `app` (the default) serves everything; `data` is the data server (`scripture.tips`), `DataModule`, which serves only the widget routes (`/api/widget/*`) and needs only `DATABASE_URL`: no Valkey, SMTP or file store, no accounts, no cookies. Every other route is a 404 there. **`ACCOUNTS`** (instance policy): `open` (the default) or `closed`, an app instance with only the reader's public routes (`ReaderModule`: translations and passages, search, study words, the widget), with no Valkey, SMTP or file store, until accounts open (vision §10, extension release). Tests: `src/roles.test.ts`. Any other value of either stops the API at start. Production image: infra/docker/api, run by infra/deploy.
- **Settings** come from the environment (`src/auth/config.ts`, `src/pdf-builds/settings.ts`; compose.yaml sets them for development). Tests pass their own: `testAccounts()` gives a memory store, a list of sent mail and an easy bot check; `testBuilds()` a memory file store and a queue the test finishes jobs on.
- **Errors are codes, never text** (`unknown-translation`, `no-such-chapter`, `too-many-verses`, `invalid-request` …), with the index where a reference went wrong. The UI words them in the user's language.
- **Compiled, not run from source.** Node can't run decorators from TypeScript, so `tsc -p tsconfig.build.json` writes `dist/`; workspace packages are still loaded from their source. That's why only this app allows non-erasable syntax (`erasableSyntaxOnly: false`); packages Node runs from source must not use it.
- **Inject by token** (`@Inject(TranslationsService)`, `@Inject(DATABASE)`): no decorator metadata is emitted, so constructor types alone don't wire anything.
- **Validation:** Zod schemas through `ZodPipe`.
- **Running it:** `./dev up` starts it with the database, Valkey, Mailpit, Garage and the PDF worker, rebuilding and restarting on every change, at http://127.0.0.1:3001/api (the app role, reached by visitors through the web app). A second container, `api-data`, runs the same build in the data role as https://dev.scripture.tips through Caddy. Logs: `./dev logs api` or `./dev logs api-data`.
- **Tests:** integration tests through Fastify's `inject` on a test database (packages/db test support).

- **Links** (`src/links/`, domain-model §3.1), private to their owner: `POST /api/links` (`{compilation, source, target}`: `source` is canonical OSIS, `target` a reference typed in that compilation's numbering, or `{reference, translation}`, a passage picked from search results in that translation's numbering (OSIS, made canonical through its scheme; `unknown-translation`); the same source, target and quote again returns the link already there; parsed with `parseReference` and stored canonical with the entered text and scheme; one passage only, `one-passage`, and it must be in the canon, `not-in-canon`) and `GET /api/links?ref=` list the user's links with that passage at either end (any overlap; each says which `end` it was asked from and names the other), `DELETE /api/links/{id}` (someone else's is `unknown-link`, 404). The optional `quote` (the selected words) and `anchor` (`{in, compilation, start: {verse, offset}, end}`: where they're pinned, validated but not checked against the text) are stored and returned. Relationship type and note are null until their forms exist.
- **Translation reads** (`src/translations/`): `GET /api/translations`, `/:translation/books` and `/:translation/passages?ref=` are the same for everyone, so successful answers carry `Cache-Control: public` (`max-age=300` for the list and book lists, `3600` for passages). Errors don't (the header is set in the handler once there's an answer: a `@Header` decorator would put it on an error's reply too).
- **Widget** (`src/widget/`, vision §3.6): what website plugins and the browser extension read, from other sites' pages: `GET /api/widget/translations` (the translations whose license is on record) and `GET /api/widget/passage?translation=&ref=&scheme=` (`ref` in OSIS, in the numbering `scheme` names, the KJV's by default; made canonical, then read out of the translation by its canonical range, so another numbering shows the right verses; at most `MAX_WIDGET_VERSES` (12) verses as `{number, text, notes?}` in plain text with a `more` flag; `notes` (left out when a verse has none, so older readers ignore it) are the verse's translator's notes, `{at, letter, text}`: `at` the character offset of the mark in `text`, `letter` the source's caller or a, b, c … through the passage, `text` the note's words without its own reference, the passage's `label` in the translation's own numbering, and the `attribution` the license asks for). Open to any origin (`Access-Control-Allow-Origin: *`, set by hand: a plain GET with no credentials makes no preflight), cacheable (`public, max-age=300`), no cookies. A translation whose terms aren't on record is `unknown-translation`. Proxied by the web app at `/api/widget/*`.
- **Study mode** (`src/study/`): `POST /api/study/words` takes Strong's numbers (`{ strongs: ["H0430", …] }`, up to 5,000) and gives each lemma's category (`person`, `place`, `name`, `noun`, `verb`, `adjective`, `adverb`, `pronoun`, `preposition`, `conjunction`, `particle`, `other`: `categoryOf`, from the part of speech and whether it's a proper name), its dictionary form and gloss. Reference data, the same for everyone; numbers with no lemma are left out. `GET /api/study/lemma/{strong}` gives one word in full: its occurrences in the original text (its extended lemmas too) and each dictionary's entry shortened (`summaryOf`: Strong's, then BDB or Abbott-Smith), cached for an hour; 404 for a number with no lemma.

- **Search** (`src/search/`, vision §3.14): `GET /api/search?q=&kinds=&translations=&limit=` gives `reference` results (a passage the query names, through the passage service, in the first translation searched), `text` results (verses whose words match, by PostgreSQL full-text search on `verse_text.search`, English stemming, "quoted phrases", matched words flagged in `snippet` parts: no HTML) and `original` results (a Hebrew or Greek lemma by Strong's number, English gloss, or its form with or without vowel points and accents, with its occurrences). `kinds` it doesn't search come back in `unavailable`. `GET /api/search/suggest?q=` gives books and dictionary words. Public data, cacheable. The text comes from `verse_text.plain`, written by the import (`tools/scripture-import`; `pnpm --filter @scripturetips/scripture-import run index` fills what was loaded before). Limits: English stemming doesn't join irregular forms ("forgave" doesn't find "forgive"), and a stop word at the start of a quoted phrase is ignored. **Paging:** `offset` (0–500) skips verses for the next page of `text` results (the passage and the original words come only on the first); the answer has `more: true` when another page follows (one more than the page is looked for); the pages before are found again and skipped, since a page is a slice of one ordered list. **One result per verse:** a verse is listed once, from the first translation in `translations=` that has it (else the best match), so the limit counts verses and not their copies in every translation (the home page's ten places once held a verse ten times over); verses are the same one when their canonical ranges start together, and are ordered by the best copy. **Stop words:** the verse index (`verse_text.search`) is made with ScriptureTips's own text search configuration, `scripture` (migration 0021: the English stemmer with no stop word list of its own), so every word is indexed, "the", "of" and "before" too, and a "quoted phrase" matches word for word (`"that she gave"` isn't `"that he gave"`). A plain query (no quotes) leaves out only ScriptureTips's own short list, `STOP_WORDS` in `stop-words.ts` (`the`, `a`, `of`, `and`: each is in over half the verses and says nothing about which), dropped from the query by `withoutStopWords`, outside quotes only; a query of only those finds nothing. Add to the list there; the index needs no rebuild. Verses found are ranked by `ts_rank_cd`, which favors words close together. **Words across translations:** after the verses that hold every word in one translation (one result each, as before), a plain query of two or more words (no quotes, `-word` or `or`: `wordsOf` in `query.ts`) also finds verses whose translations hold the words *between* them: translations of the same canonical verse are grouped by where their canonical range starts, and the result is the translation with most of the words plus `also`, the others that add words it lacks, each with the matched words flagged. Then, room allowing, near matches (all but about a third of the words, `wordsNeeded`; every word of one or two) carry `missing`, the words no translation of the verse holds. Both come after the exact results and only fill what the limit leaves (`combinedText` in `search.service.ts`); the fields are optional, so older clients ignore them.

Run the tests: `./toolbox pnpm --filter @scripturetips/api test`

- **Favorite translations:** `GET/PUT /api/account` also carry `favoriteTranslations` (ordered catalog IDs), `otherTranslations` (the order of the rest) and `favoritesOnly`. Each ID must be a loaded translation that can be a base (`canBeBase`), none repeated or in both lists, else `translations-invalid`.
